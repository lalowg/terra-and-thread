Fibre from flax

Flax stalks are pulled from the ground rather than cut, retted, then combed into long, pale fibres with a natural sheen.

The long way round

From field to fold, in four rooms.

  1. Room 01 · The field

    It starts in the ground.

    Flax and hemp grow from seed in a single season. Cotton opens from a boll, and wool grows back on the sheep every year. Before anything else we ask who grew it, and how.

    Flax is pulled up by the root, not cut, so that none of the fibre's length is lost.

  2. Room 02 · The mill

    Fibre becomes yarn.

    Combed fibres are drawn out and twisted together. More twist makes a yarn strong and smooth; less twist keeps it soft and lofty. Every fibre asks for something slightly different.

    Linen is often spun wet, which lets the fibres lie flat and gives the yarn its quiet shine.

  3. Room 03 · The loom

    Yarn becomes cloth.

    Warp threads run the length of the cloth and weft threads cross them. Over-under-over gives a plain weave. Step the pattern along by one and you get the diagonal of a twill, like the indigo cloth of our chore jacket.

    Wool takes a different route: it is knitted, one looped row hanging from the last.

  4. Room 04 · The workroom

    Cloth becomes a garment.

    Paper patterns are nested tightly on the cloth before anything is cut, and the offcuts become pockets, facings and the selvedge-edged Market Tote. Then each piece is sewn, pressed and checked by hand.

    Every garment leaves with what it needs to be repaired. More on that below.

Made to be mended

A hole is the start of a story.

Every garment leaves our workroom with what it needs to be repaired: spare buttons, matched thread or yarn, and a scrap of its own cloth. A darn is a weave in miniature, warp first and then weft, over and under.

  1. i.

    Wear it

    Elbows, knees and pocket corners take the most strain. That is where the story shows first.

  2. ii.

    Lay the warp

    Run parallel threads across the hole, anchored well into sound stitches on either side.

  3. iii.

    Weave the weft

    Cross them at right angles, over one and under one, alternating every row.

  4. iv.

    Pass it on

    A visible mend is a mark of use, not of failure. Wear it with some pride.
